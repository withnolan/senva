02 /Two directions

One conversation. Two directions.

A possible gloves-and-glasses system, with the aspiration of conversing without holding or positioning a phone.

  • Speech → captions in glasses.
  • Manual information from gloves, together with other necessary linguistic information → research towards text or voice.

The second direction is an open research challenge. Gloves alone cannot translate a whole language.

The work ahead

Decisions before devices.

A future plan, shaped with the people it is meant to support. None of this research has started.

  1. 01

    Define the need together.

    Co-design with Deaf signers and hearing interlocutors: which situations are worth addressing, and what would useful, acceptable support look like?

  2. 02

    Challenge the hardware choice.

    Compare gloves and glasses with cameras, phones and existing resources. Consider autonomy, comfort, calibration, cost and accessibility.

  3. 03

    Choose a question we can test.

    If research justifies it, explore off-the-shelf sensors and a few LSE exercises selected with teachers. Assess feedback across different adult learners.

Evidence should decide whether to continue, change direction or leave the hardware behind.

What remains open

A signal is not a language.

What information would be missing?+

Sign languages also use facial expression, the body and space. Inertial sensors alone cannot be assumed to establish hand position relative to the body. Sensors and system architecture are still to be explored.

What could a practice experiment establish?+

Only whether selected measurements and teacher-verified feedback might be useful in a limited learning context. Beginner practice data would not establish natural-conversation translation; that requires separate research with competent signers.

Why begin with LSE?+

Spanish Sign Language (LSE) is a proposed first scope, not a universal sign language. The exercises, user needs, teaching-centre interest and value of gloves over alternatives all need their own validation.
