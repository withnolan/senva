02 /The vision

Both directions. A shared conversation.

Research concept · no prototype

We imagine a possible system of gloves and glasses to assist communication in both directions. Gloves could contribute manual signals to research on assistance from Spanish Sign Language (LSE) to speech or text, alongside other linguistic information. Glasses could display captions of the interlocutor’s speech and add sensors if their value is demonstrated.

The aspiration: more freedom to converse, without having to hold or position a phone.

This is a concept to investigate with competent signers. It does not promise perfect, universal or real-time translation. A learning experiment would not by itself establish a path to natural conversation.

03 /A first hypothesis

Communication is the purpose. Learning is a first hypothesis we will explore.

Start small.Learn what matters.

One possible first application is a pair of sensor gloves and an app for adult LSE learners. With teachers, we would investigate sensors and feedback on selected aspects of hand configuration and movement in specific practice exercises.

A possible research starting point that complements teaching. It is not a guaranteed route to translation.

04 /Questions before hardware

What would useful support look like?

Technology has to earn its place in a conversation, and in people’s lives.

Which communication needs are worth addressing, and would gloves and glasses be useful and acceptable to the people using them?
01What can the sensors actually tell us?

Which aspects of hand configuration and movement could be measured reliably? We must also explore how to establish hand position relative to the body; inertial sensors alone cannot be assumed to solve it.

02Could learning be a useful first experiment?

Could feedback justify gloves’ cost, calibration and discomfort compared with cameras and existing learning resources? Teachers would need to verify the feedback, and students would need to understand and use it. This would test a practice hypothesis, not validate conversation translation.

03What would communication assistance require?

Facial expression, body movement and the use of space carry linguistic information. Recognising hand movements is not the same as understanding a language. Any future communication research would require its own study and data from competent signers; beginner practice data would not be enough.

05 /The work ahead

Evidence before assumptions.

A proposed path from an idea to an informed decision. Co-design with Deaf signers would help define the needs worth addressing and whether the proposed hardware is useful and acceptable.

  1. 01

    Define the need together

    Plan research with Deaf signers and hearing interlocutors around communication needs. Separately, explore practice difficulties with adult LSE learners and teachers.

    ↗
  2. 02

    Challenge the hardware choice

    Compare the proposed hardware with camera-based, phone-based and existing educational alternatives for usefulness, accessibility, acceptability, comfort and cost.

    ↗
  3. 03

    Choose a first experiment

    If the research justifies it, explore a limited practice experiment with off-the-shelf sensors and a few exercises chosen with teachers.

    ↗
  4. 04

    Evaluate with people

    Involve Deaf signers in assessing usefulness and acceptability. For the practice experiment, evaluate feedback with teachers and learners, including calibration and comfort across different people.

    ↗
  5. 05

    Let the evidence decide

    Continue, change direction or set the gloves aside. A useful answer matters more than defending the original idea.

    ↗
